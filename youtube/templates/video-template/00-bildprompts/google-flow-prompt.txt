==================================================
WRITTEN STYLE LOCK — SERIOUS MINIMAL COUNTRYBALL
==================================================
Serious Minimal Countryball Explainer visual world, exactly matching the Reel visual DNA and adapted only to a 16:9 YouTube canvas. Clean serious flat 2D explainer illustration. Countryballs belong to this world and, when used, are perfectly round with simple white eyes, thick clean black outlines, controlled flat colors and restrained soft graphic shading. Adult and informative, never childish or goofy.

IMPORTANT: the visual WORLD is Countryball-based, but a Countryball CHARACTER is NOT mandatory in every image.

No normal illustrated humans. No humanoid cartoon people. No stick figures. No photorealism. No realistic human scenes. No glossy 3D. No Pixar. No clay. No anime.

==================================================
SCENE ILLUSTRATION V2 — HARD LOCK
==================================================
THIS VIDEO MUST LOOK LIKE A SERIES OF CLEAR ILLUSTRATIONS, NOT LIKE ABSTRACT INFOGRAPHIC POSTERS.

Prefer the clearest visual form for the exact sentence:
- a recognizable place, institution, environment or situation when a real scene helps
- a single object when that object explains the idea best
- a map when space, borders or geography are the point
- a document, building or institution when it is the real subject
- a short simple schema when sequence or structure is easier to understand that way
- Countryballs visibly doing something only when actors improve the explanation
- multiple relevant elements or multiple Countryballs when the sentence genuinely needs comparison, conflict or interaction
- varied but controlled scene colors

Avoid as the default:
- abstract symbol boards with no concrete topic connection
- presentation-slide layouts
- split-panel infographic posters that cram many unrelated ideas together
- multiple boxed explanations
- rows or grids of decorative icons
- giant diagram arrows floating in empty space without a clear topic-specific purpose
- tiny Countryballs surrounded by labels merely because a figure was forced into the image

A simple image is allowed. A complex image is allowed. The content decides.

==================================================
EDITORIAL STORY & VISUAL BRIDGES V2 — HARD LOCK
==================================================
SHOW THE CONSEQUENCE, NOT THE SAME PLACE OVER AND OVER. A sentence about an oil shipment may be best illustrated by a refinery, petrol station, truck, household bill, shipping insurer, port worker's equipment, or a clear chart rather than yet another image of the Strait of Hormuz. A political concept can be explained with a symbolic everyday analogy if the comparison genuinely teaches the spoken mechanism.

Do not force every frame to literally depict the video's primary location. A VISUAL CAN BE INDIRECTLY RELEVANT when the visual illustrates a stated cause, mechanism, tangible consequence, example, comparison, verified data, or an explicitly meaningful analogy. It still MUST explain its actual spoken sentence and have a clear intellectual connection to the topic. The 'Topic Anchor' may therefore describe a narrative/conceptual bridge, not merely a named place.

Plan deliberate variety of explanation modes: location and actors → mechanism → real-world effect → everyday consequence → sourced numbers → contrasting comparison → context, as appropriate to the script. Avoid repeating the same map, flag, ball or camera distance with only cosmetic changes. Do not create irrelevant filler merely to satisfy variety.

Required planning fields for EVERY individual scene:
Visual Role: topic | mechanism | consequence | everyday-example | analogy | comparison | data | context
Why This Visual: a specific explanation of the spoken point and, when applicable, its concrete impact or indirect bridge.
Visual Environment: distinct relevant location, scale, staging or view; avoid five identical environments in a row without a reason.
On-screen Text: NONE, a short German label (prefer 1–6 words), verified figures/units, or QUOTE: exact and independently verified words.

SIMPLE SHORT ON-SCREEN LABELS ARE WELCOME if they help comprehension, e.g. "TEURER TRANSPORT", "VETORECHT", "2 WEGE". Relevant verified data and units are welcome, but NEVER invent prices, percentages, chart axes or before-after values. A quote may exceed six words if it is genuine, sourced and legible, not an invented soundbite. No long explanatory sentences, text walls, pseudo lettering, English overlay or decorational info cards.

ANALOGY FRAMES ARE OPTIONAL: if chosen, 'Why This Visual' explicitly says it is a symbolic comparison ("Analogie" / "Vergleich" / "symbolisch") and relates to the narrated mechanism. Clearly distinguish real footage/data from illustration and hypotheticals. Preserve the exact SAME premium flat 2D Countryball world even in a supermarket, gas station, factory, classroom, court or bank; no realistic human figures. A countryball is never mandatory.

Do NOT present any invented event, false national actor, distorted statistic, bogus quote or fake real-world price as observed fact.

VISUAL FLEXIBILITY V1 — HARD LOCK
==================================================
COUNTRYBALLS ARE OPTIONAL PER IMAGE. DO NOT FORCE A CHARACTER INTO THE FRAME.

For every Bild NN, first choose the simplest visual form that explains BOTH the current spoken idea and the concrete video topic clearly.

Explicitly allowed visual forms inside the SAME visual world:
- one strong object only
- one building or institution only
- one document only
- one clean map only
- one short simple schema / process diagram
- one Countryball with a useful action
- several Countryballs when comparison, conflict or interaction matters
- several relevant objects when the relationship between them matters
- a complete illustrated environment or scene when context is necessary

Rules:
- Do NOT add a Countryball just to make the image look like the channel.
- Do NOT add extra props, depth layers, arrows, labels or background activity just to make the frame look more complex.
- Do NOT require foreground + midground + background when a simpler composition is clearer.
- A single centered or dominant object is allowed when it is topic-specific, visually strong and the clearest explanation.
- A short schema is allowed when it explains order, structure, hierarchy or cause/effect faster than a staged scene.
- Multiple elements in one image are allowed when they belong to ONE clear visual idea.
- Several Countryballs in one image are allowed when the narration actually involves several actors or positions.
- Simplicity is NOT a quality failure. Unnecessary complexity is a quality failure.
- Complexity is allowed only when it earns its place by improving understanding.
- Keep the same flat 2D, thick-outline, controlled-color visual DNA regardless of whether the frame contains zero, one or several Countryballs.

Decision rule:
BEST VISUAL FORM > FORCED COUNTRYBALL > FORCED COMPLEX SCENE.

TOPIC VISUAL RELEVANCE V1 — HARD LOCK
==================================================
EVERY IMAGE MUST MATCH BOTH THE CURRENT SPOKEN SENTENCE AND THE SPECIFIC VIDEO TOPIC.

Before writing each image prompt, answer internally:
1. What exactly does this sentence mean inside THIS topic?
2. Which place, institution, object, conflict, actor, historical detail or situation is specific to THIS topic?
3. What is the simplest visual form that explains it clearly?
4. Does this image need zero, one or several Countryballs?
5. Could this exact image be dropped into a different explainer with almost no change? If yes, it is too generic and must be redesigned.

Mandatory:
- every Bild NN has a written `Topic Anchor:` in the image plan
- the Topic Anchor names the concrete topic-specific element that makes the image belong to this video
- prefer topic-specific institutions, objects, places, documents, historical situations and actions over generic metaphors
- generic metaphors are allowed only when no clearer topic-specific illustration exists
- arbitrary countries, flags or national Countryballs are forbidden
- a country/flag may appear only when that country is actually part of the narration or historically necessary for the exact point
- do not use Germany, France, USA or any other country merely to make a generic concept look political
- the final image must summarize the core topic itself, not end on a generic moral that could fit another video

Example logic:
Bad: two random national Countryballs at a crossroads to represent "freedom with limits".
Better for a Liberalismus video: a clean topic-specific illustration of constitution, court and protected civic freedom. Add Countryballs only if their action improves the explanation.

==================================================
PREMIUM DESIGN LAYER V1 — HARD LOCK
==================================================
Premium means BETTER DESIGN INSIDE THE SAME VISUAL WORLD, never a style change and never forced complexity.

For every image:
- create one clear visual purpose and one dominant reading order
- deliberately balance the 16:9 canvas
- use scale contrast, overlap and layering only when they improve the idea
- use foreground / midground / background only when useful
- use one coherent main palette with normally one dominant base color and one or two controlled accent colors
- use negative space intentionally; simple space around one strong object can be correct
- use arrows, routes, borders, documents and symbols only when they carry real information
- make maps simplified but elegant, legible and spatially clear
- if text is requested, treat typography as part of the composition with strong hierarchy and clean spacing
- vary visual form, direction, scale and placement between adjacent images when useful

The result should feel polished, premium and art-directed while remaining unmistakably Serious Minimal Countryball Explainer.

Clean does NOT mean empty. Minimal does NOT mean lifeless. Premium does NOT mean realistic. Premium does NOT mean complicated.

==================================================
VISUAL STORYTELLING HARD LOCK
==================================================
Choose the visual grammar that explains the point fastest. This can be a relationship, a single object, a map, a simple schema, a document, a building, a comparison or a complete scene.

When relevant, visualize before→after, cause→effect, divided→connected, route→border→obstacle, institution→rule→effect, or one concrete action leading to another.

If a Countryball actor is used:
- perfectly round body
- simple white eyes
- thick clean black outline
- restrained expression
- tiny simple limbs only when needed for the action
- no hair, neck, realistic hands or realistic face

If a map, object, document, building, institution or simple schema explains the point more clearly, use it WITHOUT forcing a Countryball.

==================================================
ADAPTIVE IMAGE DENSITY V3 — HARD LOCK
==================================================
There is NO fixed target image count.
One image = one clear visual purpose.

Split into an additional image when the narration introduces:
- a new core idea
- cause and then consequence
- before and after
- a new place, era or perspective
- a new actor
- a separate border/map/route step
- a separate number/data idea
- a switch from explanation to example
- a simple visual that would otherwise stay on screen too long

Preferred average hold: about 4.5–7.5 seconds.
Review any planned hold above 9 seconds.
Strongly prefer a meaningful split above 11 seconds.
Global hard maximum: 16 seconds.
Do not add filler images without a new visual idea.
Do not repeat the same map or composition with only tiny cosmetic changes.

Complex information should be split across multiple images rather than crammed into one overloaded frame. Simple information should stay simple instead of being artificially expanded into a busy scene.
